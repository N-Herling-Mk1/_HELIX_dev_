Project Goals

HELIX pursues three goals. Each is a distinct proof of concept that would stand on its own, yet all three are consequences of the same partition-function spine — so a successful demonstration of any one reinforces the others.

Central identity: InfoNCE is a Boltzmann distribution — softmax denominator = Z, temperature τ = 1/β.
1 Detector data as language Foundation bet

Treat an ATLAS event as a sentence and its physics objects as tokens: events carry enough sequential and distributional structure that machinery built for text — skip-gram embeddings, contrastive pretraining, attention — transfers to detector data. The numeric-to-token discretization is the core technical step.

2 Maximum-entropy background Banked · endcap

A Jaynes/MaxEnt model of the background yields a surprise score S(x) = −log p(x) that flags signal-like events and is structurally orthogonal to the ABCD plane — a second, neural-network-free background handle. One falsification test already passed (endcap); the open question is breadth.

3 Bayesian epistemic bounds Hardest to converge

Quantify what the model cannot know: epistemic uncertainty decomposes into overall and decision-boundary components, measured against the MaxEnt floor H(pₖ). The risk here is convergence, not concept.

The thread tying them together: Jaynes → InfoNCE-as-Z → one shared spine that yields a learned event embedding (Goal 1), a complementary background handle (Goal 2), and a physics-derived epistemic floor (Goal 3).
