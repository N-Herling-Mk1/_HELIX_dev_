Motivation & Genealogy

HELIX is not one idea but a confluence of several, organized around a single hinge. This panel states that hinge — the InfoNCE = partition function identity — in full, then traces the four intellectual threads that feed into it, so the architecture reads as a lineage rather than a list.

The hinge: InfoNCE is a Boltzmann distribution

What it is. InfoNCE is a contrastive objective: given an anchor and a batch containing one matching (positive) example among many non-matching (negative) ones, the model is trained to assign the positive the highest similarity, scored through a softmax. It was introduced as the loss inside Contrastive Predictive Coding and sharpened — as the NT-Xent loss, with large negative batches and a tunable temperature — by SimCLR . It needs no labels: structure is learned from the data's own co-occurrence.

The identity HELIX is built on is algebraic. An InfoNCE softmax has the form exp(sim/τ) / Σ exp(sim/τ) — and that is exactly the shape of a Boltzmann distribution: an exponential of a (negative) energy over a normalizing sum. The denominator is the partition function Z; the temperature τ plays the role of the thermodynamic 1/β. Reading a softmax as a Boltzmann distribution is not itself new — it is recognized in the energy-based-model literature, and the softmax-as-Boltzmann observation predates this work. So HELIX does not claim to have discovered that InfoNCE looks thermodynamic.

What is established, and what is ours. Three layers, kept deliberately distinct so the contribution is honest. (1) The mechanism — InfoNCE as a training loss — is standard and published ; it is the least sacred layer, replaceable in principle by another contrastive objective. (2) The Boltzmann form — that the loss is exponential-over-partition-function — is recognized prior art, not a HELIX claim. (3) What is HELIX's is the binding: tying that Boltzmann form to the Bayesian partition function, so that one and the same Z serves as the contrastive normalizer, the Bayesian evidence, and the source of a maximum-entropy uncertainty floor — and then carrying that single object across all three project goals. The partition-function formalism that makes the Z ↔ Bayesian-evidence half rigorous, rather than a loose analogy, is Kuntz et al. , from which the information entropy, the surprise statistic, and an effective dimension all follow by differentiation. To our knowledge this specific unification — one partition function spanning the embedding, the background handle, and the epistemic floor in a collider search — has not appeared in the literature, though that is a novelty claim worth a careful citation check before it is asserted hard.

Mechanism vs. core assumption — what actually has to be defended. It is tempting to call InfoNCE the core assumption of HELIX. It is not. InfoNCE is the load-bearing wall; the foundation is the prior bet that detector data carries enough language-like structure for this machinery to transfer at all. A committee would not attack InfoNCE — it is standard and hard to dispute — it would attack the foundation. That is precisely why the Week-0 spikes (does tokenization preserve separation? does the cutflow have learnable grammar?) exist: they are the empirical test of the real core assumption, run before any week is committed to it.

Why we use it. Three reasons stack. First, InfoNCE is the natural objective once the data-as-language bet is made — it is how distributional embeddings are learned, so Evt2Vec inherits a proven recipe. Second, the softmax-as-Z form is exactly what lets Jaynes' program govern the embedding, making InfoNCE the structural hinge that turns three separate goals into one project. Third, it requires no signal-MC labels, which sidesteps HELIX's hardest practical constraint — Monte-Carlo availability.

How it has been used before. CPC established it across speech, vision, and text ; SimCLR made it the backbone of state-of-the-art self-supervised vision ; and — most directly relevant — JetCLR carried contrastive pretraining into high-energy physics, learning representations of jet constituents with a permutation-invariant transformer and evaluating them by linear-probe . That last result is the existence proof that event-level contrastive representations work on physics data, and it sets the yardstick HELIX adopts.

What we expect. Concretely, and bounded: Evt2Vec embeddings that beat raw features on a linear-probe AUC (the Week 5–6 proof-of-concept gate), train without representation collapse, and — via WEFAT — provably encode physical quantities such as displacement or mass point. This is a proof of concept, not a tuned production embedding; the claim is that the bridge holds and the representation carries physics, not that it beats GN2 on day one.

The four threads below trace where the pieces of that hinge come from. They cross-cut the three goals — the information-theoretic spine feeds all of them.

1 · The information-theoretic spine

The oldest thread, and the load-bearing one. Shannon defined information as surprise — the surprisal of an outcome is −log p(x), and its average is entropy . Jaynes then made the move HELIX is built on: thermodynamics is in essence a theory of inference, and the maximum-entropy principle selects the least-committal distribution consistent with known constraints . Recent work makes the bridge rigorous — the Bayesian evidence is a partition function, and from it the information entropy, the surprise statistic, and an effective dimension all follow by differentiation — with normalising flows giving a sampler-free way to compute it for non-Gaussian posteriors .

→ How HELIX uses it. This thread is the spine the hinge attaches to. The surprise score S(x) = −log p(x) becomes a background handle, and the MaxEnt floor H(pk) becomes a physics-derived bound on what the model can know.

2 · The language lineage

The bet that detector data is language-like has a clear ancestry. The distributional hypothesis — meaning lives in co-occurrence — became practical with word2vec, which learned skip-gram embeddings cheaply at scale . Contrastive self-supervision then generalized the idea beyond text, through InfoNCE and SimCLR , and into physics with JetCLR — the contrastive mechanics of which are treated in full in the hinge section above.

→ How HELIX uses it. Evt2Vec is word2vec for events; CutFormer is the attention stage JetCLR shows is viable on physics data. The bigram baseline measures how much structure the Markov assumption misses, and tokenization is the one genuinely new technical step.

3 · Background estimation & bias

The second goal descends from the workhorse of data-driven background estimation: the ABCD method, which rests on two variables being independent for background. Machine learning automated and stress-tested it — ABCDisCo constructs decorrelated discriminators and showed the method's accuracy hinges on low signal contamination in the control regions — while CWoLa demonstrated classifiers can be trained on real mixed samples without labels, avoiding the risk of a model learning artifacts of the simulation . A separate lineage from NLP closes the loop: word embeddings necessarily absorb the statistical regularities — including biases — of their training distribution, and WEAT/WEFAT were introduced to measure that .

→ How HELIX uses it. The MaxEnt surprise score is a second background handle, structurally orthogonal to the ABCD plane. WEAT cross-checks the independence ABCD assumes and — echoing CWoLa's concern — probes where a discriminant has learned simulation provenance rather than physics; WEFAT validates that Evt2Vec embeddings encode genuine physical quantities.

4 · The Bayesian-uncertainty lineage

The third goal asks what a model cannot know — which a point estimate hides. Dropout-as-Bayesian showed that epistemic uncertainty can be extracted cheaply from networks already in use, legitimizing practical Bayesian deep learning . The partition-function formalism ties this back to the spine: entropy and effective dimension fall out of the same Z . HELIX positions against GN2, ATLAS's transformer flavour-tagger and the current state of the art, differentiating not on raw tagging power but on quantified epistemic error .

→ How HELIX uses it. A Bayesian last layer with HMC (with the normalising-flow route in reserve ) yields overall and decision-boundary uncertainty, rendered as a posterior against the floor; the RRM-4 vector turns that floor into a stopping criterion.

A closing observation on the lineage: the HEP-ML half is a connected cluster, not scattered picks — Kasieczka co-authors both JetCLR and ABCDisCo , and Nachman both ABCDisCo and CWoLa . HELIX sits at the confluence of these established threads; its contribution is the binding of them through a single partition function. See the References for the full bibliography.
