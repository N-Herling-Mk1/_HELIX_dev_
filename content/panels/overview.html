01 · Overview

Project Overview

The central claim is an identity: the InfoNCE contrastive loss is a Boltzmann distribution — the softmax denominator is the partition function Z, and the temperature τ is the inverse of an entropy scale. That single bridge lets Jaynes’ maximum-entropy program govern both the embedding objective and the inference downstream, and places the work on the formal footing of the Bayes-partition framework .

The cutflow is read as a thermodynamic process: each selection cut is a prior update, and the entropy drop across a cut is the per-cut information gain. The architecture is two-phase — Evt2Vec (a Word2Vec analogue producing self-supervised event embeddings) feeding CutFormer (a transformer over the cutflow sequence) — positioned against ATLAS GN2 and distinguished by its information-theoretic grounding and novel epistemic metrics (overall & boundary epistemic error, the RRM-4 penalty vector).

HELIX pursues three goals. Each is a distinct proof of concept that would stand on its own, yet all three are consequences of the same partition-function spine — so a successful demonstration of any one reinforces the others. Each is framed below as a falsifiable hypothesis: a claim, the test that could break it, the evidence for and against, and the kill condition that would end it. They are not equally settled — Goal 2 already carries a banked positive result, Goal 1 is the foundation bet the Week-0 spikes exist to test, and Goal 3 is the hardest to make converge.

1

Detector data as language

Foundation bet
Claim

Treat an ATLAS event as a sentence and its physics objects as tokens: events carry enough sequential and distributional structure that machinery built for text — skip-gram embeddings, contrastive pretraining, attention — transfers to detector data and extracts discriminating power. The standing wrinkle is that we have numbers, not words: the numeric-to-token discretization is the core technical step, not a footnote.

Test
  • Cutflow-grammar bigram — do signal and background cut-transition matrices differ, or is the cutflow Markov-trivial?
  • Discretization sanity — does tokenizing the numeric features preserve signal/background separation?
  • The real gate — does an Evt2Vec linear-probe AUC beat raw-feature AUC?
Evidence for
  • binned AUC ≈ raw AUC (discretization loses nothing)
  • transition matrices structurally diverge
  • Evt2Vec linear-probe AUC measurably exceeds raw features
  • no embedding collapse
Evidence against
  • binning erases separation
  • matrices near-identical — no grammar to learn
  • Evt2Vec ≤ raw features on the probe
Kill condition

If discretization destroys separation and the bigram shows no grammar, the foundation is false — stop before Evt2Vec is ever trained. This is exactly why those two checks are Week 0, not Week 5.

Not fatal to the project: Goals 2 and 3 do not strictly require Evt2Vec, so a Goal-1 failure costs the headline unification but leaves HELIX standing as “MaxEnt background + Bayesian bounds.” Framing default — flip if the committee posture is that Goal 1 is load-bearing.

2

Maximum-entropy background

Banked · endcap
Claim

A Jaynes/MaxEnt model of the background yields a surprise score S(x) = −log p(x) that flags signal-like events and is structurally orthogonal to the ABCD plane — a second, neural-network-free background handle that does not depend on the discriminant. This goal also carries the human-bias bridge: WEAT/WEFAT adapted to probe where a discriminant has learned simulation artifacts rather than physics.

Test
  • correlation r(S, NN) in each region vs. the ABCD reference |rD|
  • agreement of the surprise-defined region with ABCD region A
  • the barrel-Poisson sniff
Evidence for
  • Endcap: ≈97% agreement with ABCD region A, r(S, NN1) ≈ −0.0009 — essentially independent. Banked pass.
  • Barrel: r(S, NN2) falls from 0.27 toward |rD| = 0.055 under Poisson priors on the counts.
Evidence against
  • surprise correlates strongly with the NN everywhere — not orthogonal, just re-deriving the discriminant
  • barrel stays at r ≈ 0.27 even with correct priors — orthogonality is region-specific and fragile
Kill condition

If S(x) cannot be made orthogonal to the NN in any region, it is not an independent handle and Goal 2 collapses to “redundant with the classifier.” The endcap result already rules this out — so the live risk is breadth (how many regions), not existence.

3

Bayesian epistemic bounds

Hardest to converge
Claim

Quantify what the model cannot know: epistemic uncertainty decomposes into overall and decision-boundary components, can be measured against the MaxEnt floor H(pk) , and that comparison tells you whether a model has hit its epistemic limit.

Test
  • BLL-HMC convergence (R̂ ≈ 1, healthy ESS) yielding σoverall and σboundary
  • the posterior rendered against the floor H(pk)
  • the normalising-flow route as a convergence-proof fallback
Evidence for
  • HMC converges and produces stable σ’s
  • the posterior sits at or above the floor, as theory predicts
  • flow-based entropy agrees with HMC entropy — cross-method validation (Röspel agreeing with Kuntz)
Evidence against
  • HMC will not converge (the mk2 ESS = 5, R̂ = 9–189 collapse)
  • σ’s unstable across reruns
  • the posterior violates the floor — which would mean the floor is mis-derived, not that the model is good
Kill condition

Harder to kill outright. But if neither HMC nor flows produce stable epistemic estimates, the “quantify what the model cannot know” claim is unsupported and Goal 3 degrades from diagnostic to merely descriptive.

The thread tying them together: Jaynes → InfoNCE-as-Z → one shared spine that yields a learned event embedding (Goal 1), a complementary background handle (Goal 2), and a physics-derived epistemic floor (Goal 3).
